\begin{abstract}
Confidence scores from single-cell perturbation predictors are often treated
as if they preserve the same ordering of easy and hard perturbations across
biological settings. We separate this question from a confounding source:
whether each target condition was refit. In a controlled Frangieh ladder, a
source-context predictor is fit once per shared perturbation-label fold and
scored unchanged against three target outcomes. Across three fidelity
definitions, matched perturbations show source-frozen rank displacement, with
pairwise inversion rates ranging from $0.10$ to $0.32$ across source contexts.
Their response programs also shift strongly (mean $1-\cos$ distance
$=0.79$--$0.85$), but that response displacement is descriptive and does not
imply a causal mechanism. A matched raw-cell audit uses 30 independent
half-splits and pair-specific cell budgets. The resulting cross-context rank
displacement is not consistently above the joint measurement-plus-model noise
floor: the macro mean $\Delta_{\mathrm{joint}}$ is $-0.009$ for delta cosine,
$+0.006$ for Systema centroid accuracy and $-0.036$ for absolute-effect rank
agreement, with bootstrap intervals generally crossing zero. The pre-specified
real $n\geq40$ sensitivity gives $-0.009$, $+0.006$ and $-0.034$ for the same
metrics, respectively, preserving this direction and interval interpretation.
The previously
reported context-adapted OOF inversion is therefore retained as secondary
deployment evidence, not as a frozen-predictor causal diagnostic. A train-only
monotonic Platt recalibration changes probability calibration while producing
zero AURC change and zero strict-order disagreements across 45
environment--predictor groups.
An outcome-blind second matched-context audit in Nadig HepG2/Jurkat covers
2,392 shared perturbations and reaches the same qualitative boundary: across
all three metrics and both cell-count thresholds, cross-context displacement
shows no stable excess over the matched joint noise floor. PTL is evaluated
later as a deployment-safe probe, with transport evidence reported as
heterogeneous rather than portable by default.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Single-cell perturbation models turn large screens into predictions that can
prioritize experiments over a combinatorial intervention space. Their value
therefore depends not only on average fidelity, but on whether a researcher
can decide which predictions to trust before spending experimental budget.
Predictors span matching, linear, generative and graph-based models such as
\gears{}~\citep{norman2019manifolds,lotfollahi2019scgen,bunne2023cellot,lotfollahi2023cpa,roohani2023gears};
an uncertainty score learned in K562 need not mean the same thing in RPE1,
iPSC-derived neurons or patient-derived melanoma.

Accuracy alone does not settle this decision problem. Deep perturbation
predictors do not yet reliably outperform simple linear baselines, while
Systema and scPertEval show that reference construction, split design and
metric choice can change conclusions~\citep{ahlmann2025simple,wong2025simple,systema2025,scperteval2026}.
The ordering of trusted versus risky predictions is therefore an object in
its own right, not an afterthought to one benchmark score.

Calibration, selective prediction and shift audits turn scores into decisions
\citep{guo2017calibration,geifman2017selective,ovadia2019uncertainty,liang2024selective},
but usually ask whether a score tracks error in one task or whether a
representation transports. They do not establish that the identity of easy
and hard perturbations survives a biological change~\citep{jalaldoust2024transport}.
We study that narrower operational question without observing target outcomes.

Our central object is the identifiability boundary for reliability reordering:
\begin{quote}
\emph{Source-frozen predictions can show substantial cross-context rank
displacement, but a valid excess-reordering claim must exceed a matched
measurement-plus-model noise floor. We therefore separate observed rank
instability from identifiable context-specific excess, audit replication
eligibility before looking at risk, and test whether monotonic recalibration
can repair the ordering.}
\end{quote}
Before asking whether confidence transports across biological contexts, one
must first establish that the reliability ordering it is supposed to track is
reproducible above experimental measurement noise.
We use \emph{Reliability Shift} only as an umbrella term for the broader
difficulty, ranking and calibration audits.

We make four contributions. First, source-frozen reliability orderings vary
substantially across matched biological contexts, while matched raw-cell
resampling produces instability of comparable magnitude. Second, this
measurement-limited boundary reproduces in an outcome-blind Nadig
HepG2/Jurkat audit across three fidelity definitions. Third, monotone
recalibration changes probability scale but cannot repair an unstable ordering.
Fourth, prospective transport remains heterogeneous, so PTL is reported as a
deployment-side probe rather than a universal reliability estimator.

\section{Observed reordering is bounded by measurement reliability}
For biological instance $i$, let $\hat y_i$ be a predictor output and $y_i$
the observed delta-expression response. Fidelity $F_i$ is computed from the
prediction and response, and continuous risk is $R_i=1-F_i$. A binary label is
used only to calibrate selective diagnostics,
\begin{equation}
 T_i = \mathbb{I}\left[F_i \geq \tau\,\operatorname{median}
 (F_{\mathrm{validation}})\right], \qquad \tau=0.8.
\end{equation}
The threshold is fit on validation perturbations separately for each
predictor--environment surface. Final transfer analyses use continuous risk
and AURC, so a target environment is not tuned with its own outcomes.

For coverage $c$, selective risk is the mean risk among the highest-scored
$c$ fraction. AURC is the integral of this curve; lower is better. We report
environment-first averages, paired hierarchical bootstrap intervals, risk at
50/80\% coverage, false-trust rate, Brier score, AUROC and AUPRC.

\subsection{Same confidence, different realized risk}
We fit predictor-relative confidence bins on calibration rows and apply them
unchanged to held-out test rows. Conditional grouping loss, matched-confidence
risk gaps and their hierarchical environment--instance bootstrap intervals
quantify residual heterogeneity after confidence conditioning; the audit
never turns measured target outcomes into deployment features. Full grouping
definitions and the matched-confidence null are in the supplement.

\subsection{Controlled Shift Ladder: difficulty and ranking components}
The controlled Frangieh ladder is the Claim Lock diagnostic: common labels are
scored by a source predictor held fixed while only the target outcome changes.
Its 243-label five-fold surface shows source-frozen pairwise inversions of
$0.10$--$0.32$ and normalized rank displacement of $0.073$--$0.229$ across
three contexts and three fidelity definitions. Response programs also shift
($1-\cos=0.79$--$0.85$), but the label-level association is weak
($\rho=0.04$--$0.17$) and is descriptive, not causal.

The context-adapted OOF surface (243--246 labels; $0.248$--$0.368$ inversions)
is secondary because each condition is refit. The source-frozen primary uses
30 independent raw-cell half-splits, independent control splits and the
pair-specific $\lfloor\min(n_{p,e_1},n_{p,e_2})/2\rfloor$ budget. The macro
$\Delta_{\mathrm{joint}}$ is $-0.009$, $+0.006$ and $-0.036$ for delta cosine,
Systema and effect-rank; the $n\geq40$ sensitivity gives $-0.009$, $+0.006$
and $-0.034$. All paired intervals cross zero. Confidence tracking is
$0.122$--$0.152$ versus a permutation null of $0.249$--$0.251$. Thus rank
instability is observable, but a stable excess above joint noise is not.
The complete estimands and secondary metrics are supplied in the supplement.

The comparison uses shared perturbations across control, co-culture and
IFN$\gamma$; degenerate mean-matching confidence is not counted as support.
The matched ladder is a within-study microscope that complements the broad
eight-environment surface; coverage limits remain explicit in the audit.

\subsection{Response reprogramming across modality, time and cell context}
The Frangieh OOF surface is the primary trained-predictor evidence. The matched
head-to-head resource~\citep{headtohead2026crispr} is an orthogonal,
evaluation-only source-response persistence baseline, not a trained predictor
and not pooled with Frangieh. Modality, time and cell-context comparisons keep
exact target intersections fixed; across six contrasts they contain 22--25
targets, with 12.0--36.4\% risk-order inversions and $0.66$--$0.97$ response
shifts. This shows that the measured response object changes, not that every
trained model must reorder or become uncalibratable. Full coverage and
guide-semantics audits are in the supplement.

\section{Replication boundary and calibration safeguard}
An outcome-blind registry scanned 33 local same-study file pairs. Nadig
HepG2--Jurkat was the only eligible overlap after checking shared labels,
controls, RNA readout, guide signatures and external provenance; GEO describes
parallel screens with the same dual-sgRNA library family, day-7 harvest and
10x 3' v3 readout, with batch IDs nested within context. Replogle
K562-essential--RPE1 remains confounded by inseparable day-6 versus day-7
harvest, and no risk or inversion entered selection. The fixed Claim Lock was
run once on Nadig (2,392 shared perturbations; 6,606 genes; 30 raw-cell splits;
2,000 paired bootstrap draws), retaining 1,255 labels at $n\geq20$ and 345 at
$n\geq40$. Across both thresholds and all three metrics, the joint-floor
intervals cross zero. The audit therefore reproduces the identifiability
boundary, not a universal transport claim. Full provenance and guide-semantics
decisions are in the supplement.

\subsection{Recalibration cannot repair reordered reliability}
The calibration-side scalar score is mapped by a train-only monotonic Platt
model and evaluated on untouched test rows. It changes Brier score in some
groups but preserves every comparable order: all 45 groups have zero strict
order disagreements and zero AURC change. A strictly increasing map preserves
strict order exactly; a non-decreasing map may add ties but cannot reverse one.
Our implementation asserts the fitted Platt slope $a>0$ in
$g(C)=\sigma(aC+b)$. Thus probability-scale recalibration cannot explain the
ordering change; richer non-monotonic predictors remain outside this test.

\section{Deployment consequence: prospective reliability transport is difficult}
Let $d_{st}$ denote outcome-free descriptors between source environment $s$
and target environment $t$. The evaluated descriptor vector contains exactly
the frozen fields: same cell context, modality, platform, condition, dataset
family, context+modality, geometry distance and uncertainty-distribution
distance. These descriptors are measured before target outcomes are available;
we do not claim that they identify all useful biological similarity.

The transport problem is source selection, not response prediction. We fit a
small gain model on source--target pairs for which outcomes are available in
the training portion and evaluate it with strict leave-target-environment-out
cross-validation. Target outcomes are never used to build $d_{st}$ or select a
source during the held-out evaluation. Performance is assessed by gain-rank
correlation, positive-transfer discrimination, source-selection regret and
calibration against a permutation null.

\begin{equation}
 r_s(x)=f_{\theta}(U,P,S,N,C), \qquad
 r_U(x)=f_{\theta}(U),
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
 g(s,t)=h_{\psi}(d_{st}), \qquad
 \operatorname{prospective\_gate}(s,t)=
 \begin{cases}
 r_s, & \widehat g(s,t)>0 \text{ and } \Pr(g>0\mid d_{st})\geq\eta,\\
 r_U, & \text{otherwise.}
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
We call this a prospective gating baseline rather than a safeguard: it is an
auditable deployment heuristic, not a demonstrated protective method. Its
fallback is deliberately asymmetric, returning U-only when the fixed
validation-only acceptance criterion is not met.

\section{Deployment-safe PTL probe}
\subsection{Deployment-safe feature surface}
The benchmark keeps predictions, deployment features, observed outcomes and
split assignments separate. The headline feature vector contains five blocks:
$U$ (five uncertainty features), $P$ (four prediction-geometry features), $S$
(three training-support features), $N$ (four novelty features) and $C$ (five
declared context features). All normalizers, nearest-neighbor transforms and
categorical encoders are fit on calibration-side rows only. Environment and
predictor identifiers, fidelity, continuous risk, realized cell counts,
reference membership, QC outcomes and target reproducibility are excluded
from the headline feature matrix.

\subsection{Fair baseline and prospective gating baseline}
The headline PTL estimator is a random forest over $U+P+S+N+C$; the U-only
baseline has the same estimator capacity and uses only $U$. The information
ladder adds blocks in the order
$U\rightarrow U+P\rightarrow U+P+S\rightarrow U+P+S+N\rightarrow
U+P+S+N+C$. Prospective source selection is evaluated separately from the
row-level reliability score and falls back exactly to U-only when its fixed
validation-only rule is not met.

\section{Generality and robustness}
\subsection{Benchmark and protocol}
The benchmark registry contains 1,603 condition-level biological instances
across Norman K562, Replogle RPE1, Tian iPSC-derived-neuron CRISPRa and CRISPRi,
three Frangieh patient-derived-melanoma conditions, and Xu HEK293 CRISPRi.
Adamson multi-accession and Replogle K562 GWPS surfaces remain supplementary
because their pinned processed surfaces do not meet the headline coverage
contract; QC thresholds and reference matching are not relaxed. The shared
scoring panel contains 8,229 genes, while each predictor retains its native
training gene space.

All completed predictor families use split seed 20260907 and model seeds
$0,1,2$. The protocol is 60\% predictor training, 20\% reliability
calibration and 20\% untouched final test, grouped by biological instance.
The completed roster comprises matching mean, an Ahlmann--Eltze-style
bilinear model with $K=10$ and $\lambda=0.1$, and \slim{} with a public STRING
embedding.

As an external-contract audit rather than a headline comparison, the official
pinned \gears{} stack was run remotely on the Norman K562, Replogle K562 GWPS
and Replogle RPE1 surfaces for seeds $0,1,2$. Each run used 20 epochs, the
official condition-disjoint split, a dataset-frozen 4,096-gene panel, finite
native uncertainty outputs and persisted validation predictions. This
validation-backed audit enters the Reliability Shift evidence bundle for the
two supported environments; Replogle K562 GWPS remains a test-only external
audit and is outside the headline reliability surface.

Table~\ref{tab:formal} shows why a single pooled gain would be misleading.
PTL is better in the point estimate in all three scenarios, but the
  environment-holdout result is not statistically established. The paired
  2,000-draw interval for PTL versus U-only is $[-0.0424,0.0044]$ in the latter
  setting and crosses zero; we therefore do not claim general superiority.
PTL versus raw normalized UQ is a secondary comparison and remains favorable
in-domain, while the transfer atlas exposes the cases where it does not.
The direct five-split contrast between the environment-holdout and
predictor-holdout deltas is $+0.0025$ with a split-level range of
$[-0.0063,+0.0067]$. Thus the apparent holdout asymmetry is not established;
we retain the comparison as a diagnostic rather than elevating it to a
portability theorem.

\subsection{Transport heterogeneity and robustness}
Across 280 source--target observations from five split seeds, the strict
  target-unseen predictor achieved positive-transfer AUROC 0.503, with a
  1,000-permutation mean of 0.456. Ungated negative transfer was 36.4\%. The
prospective gating baseline fell back to U-only in 10\% of 40 target-by-split
decisions; among non-fallback decisions, selected-source negative transfer
was 41.7\%. Mean realized gain versus target-excluded U-only was +0.0012,
with 21 targets helped, 4 unchanged and 15 harmed. Because selected-source
harm is higher than the ungated candidate-pair rate, the baseline is evidence
that this natural heuristic is insufficient, not a protective method. We
therefore report within-target rank concordance, candidate-positive fraction
and empirical permutation p-values as the primary source-selection diagnostics.

Metric robustness is high but not identical: the released robustness bundle
reuses the saved PTL scores and recomputes selective outcomes under the pinned
Systema entrypoint and the declared alternative diagnostics, without
per-metric refitting. The fixed-budget analysis evaluates the same learned
scores at 20, 50 and 80\% budgets without budget-specific retraining. These
audits qualify the Reliability Shift result; they are not additional training
or a pooled superiority claim. Under the pinned Systema centroid-AURC
entrypoint, PTL is $0.44381$ versus $0.44279$ for U-only, with the same mean
risk ($0.471253$); this near-tie is reported as robustness evidence, not a
method win.

The Claim Lock separates this broad robustness bundle from the controlled
Frangieh diagnostic. The source-frozen artifact stores the complete prediction
vectors for the three source contexts, the shared five-fold assignment, the
three model members and the 8,229-gene checksum. The raw-cell lock then uses
the same vectors with independent half-A/B outcomes, matched cell budgets and
30 split seeds. The primary confirmatory deltas compare cross-context rank
displacement with the larger of the two target-context floors. None of the
three metrics has a consistently positive macro delta over the joint floor.
This is the appropriate boundary for the present evidence: context-dependent
reordering is observable, but its excess over measurement plus model noise is
not established: all three primary and sensitivity joint-floor intervals cross
zero. Nadig is therefore one bounded same-predictor audit, not universal
transportability. Tian has only one shared label and the head-to-head resource
is an evaluation-only persistence baseline, so neither is a replication claim.

The five-seed stability atlas and the Norman/RPE1 split-half audit characterize
rather than tune transport. They show heterogeneous gains, nonzero negative
transfer and a coverage-limited reproducibility interaction ($\Delta=-0.007$);
reproducibility is never entered into PTL. Full secondary metrics and
coverage tables are supplied in the supplement, not used for a new superiority
claim.

\section{Discussion}
The experiments support a limitation-centered view. Across two matched-context
audits, observed ordering changes do not stably exceed the joint instability
of model fitting and ground-truth measurement. PTL can improve familiar-setting
ranking, but environment holdout is not statistically established and the
prospective gate is not protective: its selected-source harm is not below the
ungated rate. Before confidence is transported, the ordering it tracks must be
shown reproducible above experimental noise; here that condition is not met.
All headline PTL features remain target-outcome-free and explicit-ID-free.
The second audit does not rescue a context-causal interpretation; it reproduces
the same identifiability boundary under a different matched study design. This
distinction matters operationally: a high-confidence prediction can still be
useful for prioritization, but its ranking should not be treated as transportable
without a prospective measurement plan.

\section{Related work and positioning}
The first dispute is evaluative: deep predictors do not yet reliably beat
simple baselines, while Systema and scPertEval show that references, splits and
metrics can change conclusions~\citep{ahlmann2025simple,wong2025simple,systema2025,scperteval2026}.
These works do not ask whether the same perturbations remain easy or hard after
a matched biological shift; our paired ordering question is complementary.

The second line concerns context and generalization. Benchmarks, context-aware
virtual-cell proposals, Frangieh and matched CRISPRko/CRISPRi screens establish
that responses vary across biological settings~\citep{generalizable2025benchmark,dibaeinia2026context,molina2026decomposition,frangieh2021multimodal,headtohead2026crispr}.
A response shift is not itself a reliability shift: our Frangieh OOF ladder
tests trained-model ordering, while the external ladder is only a persistence
baseline.

The third line concerns calibration, selective prediction and transport.
Calibration and abstention maps, shift audits, grouping loss, epistemic
criteria, PRESCRIBE and PertEMA improve score interpretation
\citep{guo2017calibration,geifman2017selective,ovadia2019uncertainty,liang2024selective,perezlebel2023grouping,melo2026epistemic,prescribe2025,shrestha2026pertema}.
Our object is different: whether a reliability ordering transports across
contexts, including what monotonic recalibration cannot repair.

\section{Limitations and reproducibility}
The claim is bounded by the eight-environment surface, three predictor families
and one Nadig same-predictor Claim Lock; Nadig is not universal replication.
Although its external study design supports independent biological contexts,
the audit cannot remove every laboratory or processing difference and does not
identify a causal mechanism. The official \gears{} run is a separate
condition-disjoint, dataset-native audit, and GWPS remains coverage-limited and
test-only. Broader model coverage and a universal assay-reproducibility claim
remain future work; the atlas is descriptive, not causal, and the gate retains
negative transfers.

All outputs are regenerated from fixed splits and native predictor spaces. The
release contains prediction contracts, deployment features, bootstrap intervals,
transfer and robustness tables, source-frozen vectors, matched measurement /
joint floors, replication manifests, split-half audits and editable vector
figures with source-data manifests. Claims are downgraded when intervals,
stability or metric robustness do not support them.

The supplement provides the complete estimands, outcome-blind registry fields,
external provenance decisions and secondary metric tables needed to reproduce
the compressed main-text claims.
