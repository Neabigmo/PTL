\begin{abstract}
Confidence scores from single-cell perturbation predictors are often treated
as if they preserve the same ordering of easy and hard perturbations across
biological settings. We show, in a controlled Frangieh condition ladder, that
matched perturbations undergo 25--43\% risk-order inversions across control,
IFN$\gamma$ and TIL co-culture conditions across five split seeds. Their
response programs also shift strongly (mean $1-\cos$ine $=0.79$--$0.85$),
but that response displacement is descriptive and does not imply a causal
mechanism. A train-only monotonic Platt recalibration changes probability
calibration while producing zero AURC change and zero strict-order
disagreements across 45 environment--predictor groups. Thus the central
failure is an ordering problem, not merely a probability-scale problem.
The broad formal benchmark contains 1,603 condition-level biological instances
from eight semantically verified environments, three completed predictor
families, an 8,229-gene shared evaluation panel, and a frozen 60/20/20
biological-instance split. PTL is evaluated later as a deployment-safe probe;
its transport evidence remains heterogeneous rather than being used to
overstate portability.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Single-cell perturbation models turn large screens into predictions of
cellular responses. Their practical value depends on whether a user can
decide which predictions to trust before the corresponding experiment is
performed. Predictors span matching baselines, linear perturbation models,
generative models and graph neural networks such as \gears{}~\citep{norman2019manifolds,lotfollahi2019scgen,bunne2023cellot,lotfollahi2023cpa,roohani2023gears}.
Each model may expose an uncertainty score, but a score learned in a K562
screen need not have the same meaning in RPE1, iPSC-derived neurons or
patient-derived melanoma.

We study a narrower operational question: when can the ordering of reliability
itself be transported to a new biological setting without observing target
outcomes?
The answer must separate deployment evidence from outcome evidence. Target
fidelity, ground truth, QC and reproducibility are appropriate for evaluation;
they are not available when a new perturbation is deployed. Conversely,
prediction geometry, training support, novelty and declared assay context can
be available before the target response is known.

Our central object is Reliability Shift:
\begin{quote}
\emph{Biological context can rewrite which matched perturbations are easy or
hard. We diagnose this as reliability reordering, separate response-program
displacement from outcome-derived risk, and test whether monotonic
recalibration can repair the ordering.}
\end{quote}

We make four contributions. First, we turn Frangieh into a controlled
condition-shift ladder with a matched-perturbation inversion estimand and a
response-program displacement audit. Second, we show with a monotonic
recalibration counterfactual that probability calibration cannot repair a
strict reliability ordering. Third, we retain the eight-environment telescope,
its canonical identities, difficulty/ranking decomposition and coverage-limited
Tian modality contrast. Fourth, we compare PTL with an equal-capacity U-only
probe, audit prospective source selection and modern-model validity, and retain
negative transport evidence rather than tuning it away.

\section{Reliability Shift}
For biological instance $i$, let $\hat y_i$ be a predictor output and $y_i$
the observed delta-expression response. Fidelity $F_i$ is computed from the
prediction and response, and continuous risk is $R_i=1-F_i$. A binary label is
used only to calibrate selective diagnostics,
\begin{equation}
 T_i = \mathbb{I}\left[F_i \geq \tau\,\operatorname{median}
 (F_{\mathrm{validation}})\right], \qquad \tau=0.8.
\end{equation}
The threshold is fit on validation perturbations separately for each
predictor--environment surface. Final transfer analyses use continuous risk
and AURC, so a target environment is not tuned with its own outcomes.

For coverage $c$, selective risk is the mean risk among the highest-scored
$c$ fraction. AURC is the integral of this curve; lower is better. We report
environment-first averages, paired hierarchical bootstrap intervals, risk at
50/80\% coverage, false-trust rate, Brier score, AUROC and AUPRC.

\subsection{Same confidence, different realized risk}
We fit predictor-relative confidence bins on calibration-side scores and apply
those fixed bins to untouched test rows. Within each bin we report the global
risk, the between-environment risk variance, the maximum environment gap and a
weighted absolute deviation of environment means from the global bin mean,
which we call \emph{contextual grouping loss}. Matched-confidence pairs are
formed from the bin assignment alone; their outcome differences are measured
  only afterward. A hierarchical environment--biological-instance bootstrap
  provides uncertainty. This makes the title's phenomenon quantitative without
  turning measured target outcomes into deployment features.
  The corrected grouping-loss analysis aggregates within-bin quantities rather
  than recomputing them on the unconditioned test frame. It reports conditional
  contextual grouping loss, matched-confidence same- and cross-environment
  risk gaps, their difference, and a confidence-bin-weighted maximum gap, with
  hierarchical bootstrap intervals. These statistics quantify residual
  heterogeneity after confidence conditioning without treating the statistic as
  a new generic calibration concept.

\subsection{Difficulty, ranking and semantic components}
The controlled Frangieh ladder provides the paper's primary diagnostic. For
each condition pair we retain only shared perturbation labels and count strict
pairwise reversals of continuous risk. On the strong-linear/PTL surface, the
five-seed mean inversion rates are $0.34$ for control $\rightarrow$ co-culture,
$0.28$ for control $\rightarrow$ IFN$\gamma$ and $0.31$ for co-culture
$\rightarrow$ IFN$\gamma$; across all completed predictor families the observed
range is $0.25$--$0.43$. The same matched labels have response-program shifts
of $0.79$--$0.85$ in mean $1-\cos$ine distance, while the split-level
response-shift/risk-change association changes sign across pairs and predictors.
We therefore use response displacement to diagnose that the biological object
has moved, not to claim that it causes a particular reliability reversal.

The recalibration counterfactual makes a sharper separation. The existing
calibration-side scalar score is mapped by a train-only monotonic Platt model
and evaluated on the untouched test rows. The mapping changes Brier score in
some groups, but it preserves every comparable pairwise order: all 45 groups
have zero order disagreements and zero AURC change. Isotonic regression is
reported separately because its monotonic ties can alter tie handling. This
rules out probability-scale recalibration as an explanation for the observed
strict ordering change; it does not establish that a richer non-monotonic
predictor would solve transport.

The decomposition makes the base-risk alternative explicit. Difficulty shift is
the variation of $\mu_e=\mathbb{E}[R\mid E=e]$ across environments. Ranking
shift is measured within each environment by Spearman and Kendall concordance
between confidence and $-R$, together with environment-normalized selective
AURC. Semantic/calibration shift is tested, but not presumed, by comparing the held-out error of
$R=\alpha_e+h(c)$ with $R=\alpha_e+h_e(c)$, where confidence bins are defined
on calibration rows and biological instances are the held-out units. We report
the interaction improvement, hierarchical interval and an environment-wise
permutation null. In the present data, difficulty shift and ranking
heterogeneity are established, whereas the semantic interaction is not
consistently supported; the claim is therefore limited to the identifiable
difficulty and ranking components.

The controlled Frangieh comparison uses shared perturbations across control,
co-culture and IFN$\gamma$. Tian has only one shared perturbation label between
CRISPRa and CRISPRi in the frozen surface, so that modality contrast is
reported as coverage-limited rather than fit as a controlled interaction. The
primary mean-matching surface has degenerate confidence for all three
Frangieh method panels, so its ranking and centered-mapping entries are NaN and
are not counted as support. The
matched Frangieh surfaces provide a microscope for condition changes within
study, complementing the eight-environment telescope. Results and the explicit
coverage status are reported in the controlled-shift manifest and are not
pooled into a stronger claim than the shared perturbation coverage supports.

\section{Prospective reliability transport is difficult}
Let $d_{st}$ denote outcome-free descriptors between source environment $s$
and target environment $t$. The evaluated descriptor vector contains exactly
the frozen fields: same cell context, modality, platform, condition, dataset
family, context+modality, geometry distance and uncertainty-distribution
distance. These descriptors are measured before target outcomes are available;
we do not claim that they identify all useful biological similarity.

The transport problem is source selection, not response prediction. We fit a
small gain model on source--target pairs for which outcomes are available in
the training portion and evaluate it with strict leave-target-environment-out
cross-validation. Target outcomes are never used to build $d_{st}$ or select a
source during the held-out evaluation. Performance is assessed by gain-rank
correlation, positive-transfer discrimination, source-selection regret and
calibration against a permutation null.

\begin{equation}
 r_s(x)=f_{\theta}(U,P,S,N,C), \qquad
 r_U(x)=f_{\theta}(U),
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
 g(s,t)=h_{\psi}(d_{st}), \qquad
 \operatorname{prospective\_gate}(s,t)=
 \begin{cases}
 r_s, & \widehat g(s,t)>0 \text{ and } \Pr(g>0\mid d_{st})\geq\eta,\\
 r_U, & \text{otherwise.}
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
We call this a prospective gating baseline rather than a safeguard: it is an
auditable deployment heuristic, not a demonstrated protective method. Its
fallback is deliberately asymmetric, returning U-only when the fixed
validation-only acceptance criterion is not met.

\section{PTL}
\subsection{Deployment-safe feature surface}
The benchmark keeps predictions, deployment features, observed outcomes and
split assignments separate. The headline feature vector contains five blocks:
$U$ (five uncertainty features), $P$ (four prediction-geometry features), $S$
(three training-support features), $N$ (four novelty features) and $C$ (five
declared context features). All normalizers, nearest-neighbor transforms and
categorical encoders are fit on calibration-side rows only. Environment and
predictor identifiers, fidelity, continuous risk, realized cell counts,
reference membership, QC outcomes and target reproducibility are excluded
from the headline feature matrix.

\subsection{Fair baseline and prospective gating baseline}
The headline PTL estimator is a random forest over $U+P+S+N+C$; the U-only
baseline has the same estimator capacity and uses only $U$. The information
ladder adds blocks in the order
$U\rightarrow U+P\rightarrow U+P+S\rightarrow U+P+S+N\rightarrow
U+P+S+N+C$. Prospective source selection is evaluated separately from the
row-level reliability score and falls back exactly to U-only when its fixed
validation-only rule is not met.

\section{Experiments}
\subsection{Benchmark and protocol}
The benchmark registry contains 1,603 condition-level biological instances
across Norman K562, Replogle RPE1, Tian iPSC-derived-neuron CRISPRa and CRISPRi,
three Frangieh patient-derived-melanoma conditions, and Xu HEK293 CRISPRi.
Adamson multi-accession and Replogle K562 GWPS surfaces remain supplementary
because their pinned processed surfaces do not meet the headline coverage
contract; QC thresholds and reference matching are not relaxed. The shared
scoring panel contains 8,229 genes, while each predictor retains its native
training gene space.

All completed predictor families use split seed 20260907 and model seeds
$0,1,2$. The protocol is 60\% predictor training, 20\% reliability
calibration and 20\% untouched final test, grouped by biological instance.
The completed roster comprises matching mean, an Ahlmann--Eltze-style
bilinear model with $K=10$ and $\lambda=0.1$, and \slim{} with a public STRING
embedding.

As an external-contract audit rather than a headline comparison, the official
pinned \gears{} stack was run remotely on the Norman K562, Replogle K562 GWPS
and Replogle RPE1 surfaces for seeds $0,1,2$. Each run used 20 epochs, the
official condition-disjoint split, a dataset-frozen 4,096-gene panel, finite
native uncertainty outputs and persisted validation predictions. This
validation-backed audit enters the Reliability Shift evidence bundle for the
two supported environments; Replogle K562 GWPS remains a test-only external
audit and is outside the headline reliability surface.

Table~\ref{tab:formal} shows why a single pooled gain would be misleading.
PTL is better in the point estimate in all three scenarios, but the
  environment-holdout result is not statistically established. The paired
  2,000-draw interval for PTL versus U-only is $[-0.0424,0.0044]$ in the latter
  setting and crosses zero; we therefore do not claim general superiority.
PTL versus raw normalized UQ is a secondary comparison and remains favorable
in-domain, while the transfer atlas exposes the cases where it does not.
The direct five-split contrast between the environment-holdout and
predictor-holdout deltas is $+0.0025$ with a split-level range of
$[-0.0063,+0.0067]$. Thus the apparent holdout asymmetry is not established;
we retain the comparison as a diagnostic rather than elevating it to a
portability theorem.

\subsection{Transport heterogeneity and robustness}
Across 280 source--target observations from five split seeds, the strict
  target-unseen predictor achieved positive-transfer AUROC 0.503, with a
  1,000-permutation mean of 0.456. Ungated negative transfer was 36.4\%. The
prospective gating baseline fell back to U-only in 10\% of 40 target-by-split
decisions; among non-fallback decisions, selected-source negative transfer
was 41.7\%. Mean realized gain versus target-excluded U-only was +0.0012,
with 21 targets helped, 4 unchanged and 15 harmed. Because selected-source
harm is higher than the ungated candidate-pair rate, the baseline is evidence
that this natural heuristic is insufficient, not a protective method. We
therefore report within-target rank concordance, candidate-positive fraction
and empirical permutation p-values as the primary source-selection diagnostics.

Metric robustness is high but not identical: the released robustness bundle
reuses the saved PTL scores and recomputes selective outcomes under the pinned
Systema entrypoint and the declared alternative diagnostics, without
per-metric refitting. The fixed-budget analysis evaluates the same learned
scores at 20, 50 and 80\% budgets without budget-specific retraining. These
audits qualify the Reliability Shift result; they are not additional training
or a pooled superiority claim. Under the pinned Systema centroid-AURC
entrypoint, PTL is $0.44381$ versus $0.44279$ for U-only, with the same mean
risk ($0.471253$); this near-tie is reported as robustness evidence, not a
method win.

The five-seed stability atlas is used to characterize, rather than tune,
transport. Across the same 56 off-diagonal pairs per baseline, the mean
transfer gain was $+0.0003$ for raw scalar UQ and $+0.0055$ for the equal-capacity
U-only baseline; negative transfer occurred in 40.7\% and 36.4\% of the
corresponding observations. A separate raw-cell split-half audit on Norman and
RPE1 yielded 245 paired perturbations with median split-half cosine 0.814; 153
of these pairs joined to the reliability surface for the risk-stratified audit.
On the primary Norman mean-matching/PTL slice, low- versus high-reproducibility
strata had mean risks $0.469$ versus $0.145$, within-stratum normalized AURCs
$0.967$ versus $0.824$, and a corrected fixed-calibration-bin grouping loss of
$0.085$ across the low/middle/high strata.  The reproducibility-stratum
interaction cross-fit improved over the additive stratum-plus-bin null by
$-0.007$, so this audit does not support a positive semantic-shift claim.
Replogle/RPE1 contributed only four joined rows (three low and one middle),
with no high-reproducibility stratum; it is therefore reported as coverage
limited rather than used for a high-versus-low inference.  Reproducibility is
used only for this evaluation/audit stratification and is not entered into
PTL.  These are stability summaries, not additional tuning or a new
superiority claim.

\section{Discussion}
The experiments support a limitation-centered view of reliability. Reliability
Shift is visible as changes in environment difficulty and within-environment
ranking; the environment-dependent semantic component is reported only to the
extent supported by its null test. PTL's additional deployment-side descriptors
improve ranking in familiar settings and under predictor-family holdout, yet
environment holdout does not produce a statistically established improvement.
The prospective gating baseline makes fallback behavior explicit, but its
selected-source harm rate is not lower than the ungated candidate-pair rate; it
is therefore not a protective safeguard.

The identity probe gives an important qualification. With explicit context
features, a grouped probe can recover environment identity very accurately;
the appropriate statement is therefore ``explicit-ID-free and
target-outcome-free,'' not unconditional identity-free prediction. Context is
allowed deployment information, but it changes what transportability means.

\section{Related work and positioning}
Generic calibration and grouping-loss analyses study whether confidence has a
consistent relationship to error, whereas our grouping-loss analysis asks
whether that relationship changes across biological environments at matched
confidence. PertEMA is a post-hoc prediction-reliability approach, while
PRESCRIBE represents predictor-native uncertainty; both are complementary to
our reliability-layer audit. Systema and scPertEval provide perturbation
evaluation protocols, and experimental perturbation-reliability studies assess
the reproducibility of measured responses. PTL's contribution here is narrower:
biological environment changes the distribution and ranking behavior of
prediction reliability, while environment-specific calibration semantics
require stronger evidence; the transport audit remains target-outcome-free.

\section{Limitations and reproducibility}
The present claim is bounded by the eight-environment headline surface and the
predictor families for which a common validation/test contract is available.
The official \gears{} execution is separately audited with a condition-disjoint
split, a dataset-native gene panel and persisted validation predictions for
Norman and RPE1; GWPS remains test-only. The audit is not used to manufacture a
cross-model superiority claim.
CPA and scGPT remain pending their pinned projects, weights
and memory-feasible execution; a 24~GB RTX 3090 is insufficient for the planned
48--80~GB modern-model surface. The GWPS local surface is
coverage-limited, and its audit explicitly prevents it from being mistaken
for a genome-wide surface. The reference-group audit is supplemented, but not
replaced, by the two-dataset raw-cell split-half audit, so it cannot support a
universal assay-reproducibility claim. Finally, the transfer predictor is near
  chance in the strict five-split audit, the prospective gate retains nonzero regret and
selected negative transfers, and the atlas is descriptive rather than causal.

All primary outputs are regenerated from the fixed split, native predictor
spaces and shared panel. The released bundle contains prediction contracts,
deployment features, paired bootstrap intervals, transfer matrices,
metric-robustness and fixed-budget tables, split-half audit summaries, and
editable vector figures with source-data manifests. Claims are downgraded
whenever confidence intervals, stability or metric robustness do not support
them.
