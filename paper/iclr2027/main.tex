\begin{abstract}
Single-cell perturbation predictors are increasingly used outside the
experimental context in which they were trained, but a model's native
uncertainty need not remain a reliable signal of prediction fidelity under
structured biological shift. We introduce a target-free reliability layer,
\ptl, that calibrates a predictor's native uncertainty using prediction
geometry, training support, perturbation novelty and explicit biological
context. The method separates predictions, deployment-time features, observed
outcomes and grouped cross-fitting assignments, so reliability decisions cannot
use the answer key. On a validated pilot replay of 23,226 prediction rows and
6,884 biological instances across three environments and three stress
families, a random-forest \ptl head reduces environment-macro AURC from 0.3790
for raw normalized uncertainty to 0.3480 and reduces the corresponding Brier
score from 0.3644 to 0.1754. These numbers are engineering and estimand
validation, not a claim of final state-of-the-art prediction. We also audit
context expansion before benchmarking: a raw Replogle GWPS screen contains
nearly two million cells but retains only 31 non-control labels after
batch-aware eligibility, whereas Tian and Frangieh metadata establish distinct
iPSC-neuron and melanoma co-culture contexts. The result is a reproducible
framework for asking when uncertainty is transportable, with explicit
boundaries for the learned-model and multi-environment experiments still to
come.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Perturbation-response models promise to turn large single-cell screens into
predictive maps of cellular state. Existing predictors span additive and
linear baselines, neural optimal-transport and generative models, and graph
neural networks such as \gears{}~\citep{norman2019manifolds,lotfollahi2019scgen,
bunne2023cellot,lotfollahi2023cpa,roohani2023gears}. Their practical use,
however, is not determined by prediction accuracy alone. A prediction may be
well supported in one cell context and extrapolative in another; a native
ensemble or model uncertainty may therefore be poorly aligned with realized
fidelity under a held-out perturbation, cell state or experimental condition.

This paper studies a narrower and operationally useful question: can a
target-free reliability layer improve selective decisions about which
predictions to retain? The distinction is important. We do not treat
reliability calibration as a new predictor, and we do not infer deployment
features from the observed target. Instead, we construct a stress-aware
evaluation surface and learn a small calibrator that can abstain from risky
predictions using information available at prediction time.

Our contributions are fourfold. First, we define an auditable contract for
prediction, deployment evidence, outcomes and grouped cross-fitting. Second,
we implement a monotonicity-preserving context-conditioned calibrator that
starts from a scalar uncertainty baseline. Third, we report a reproducible
pilot replay with environment-first aggregation and paired hierarchical
bootstrap intervals. Fourth, we show why context expansion must be audited
semantically: large raw cell counts do not guarantee broad perturbation
coverage after batch-aware eligibility.

\section{Problem formulation}
Let $x_i$ denote the perturbation input and cell context for a biological
instance, $\hat{y}_i=f(x_i)$ the predictor output, and $y_i$ the observed
delta-expression response. A predictor may provide native uncertainty
descriptors $u_i$, or we may derive predictor-relative uncertainty from aligned
ensemble members. We define a fidelity score $F_i$ from the prediction and
observed response, and a transportability label relative to a familiar/random
anchor:
\begin{equation}
 T_i = \mathbb{I}\left[F_i \geq \tau\,\operatorname{median}(F_{\mathrm{random}})\right].
\end{equation}
The label is used only in the outcome table and in training/evaluation inside
cross-fitting folds; it is not a deployment feature.

The selective task is to rank predictions by estimated reliability. For a
coverage level $c$, the selective risk is the mean realized risk among the
highest-scoring $c$ fraction. We use AURC as the primary summary and report
false-transportability rate (FTR), Brier score, log loss, AUROC and AUPRC as
secondary diagnostics. All headline pilot summaries aggregate environments
first, rather than allowing large environments to dominate the estimate.

\section{Context-conditioned reliability layer}
\subsection{Feature contract}
We maintain four logical tables. The \emph{predictions} table contains
prediction identifiers, model provenance, prediction summaries and native
uncertainty. The \emph{deployment} table contains only information available
before the target is observed: prediction geometry, training support,
perturbation novelty and biological/experimental context. The \emph{outcomes}
table contains fidelity, risk and transportability labels. The \emph{folds}
table assigns all predictions for a biological instance to the same grouped
cross-fitting fold. Tables are joined by a stable prediction identifier only
inside evaluation code. A feature audit explicitly excludes target-derived
quantities, identifiers that can memorize runs, and observed response metrics.

\subsection{Calibrator}
We first map normalized predictor uncertainty to a scalar baseline probability
$p_{0,i}$ using training-fold data only. Let $z_i$ be the concatenation of
deployment-time blocks: $U$ for uncertainty descriptors, $P$ for prediction
geometry, $S$ for support and availability, $N$ for perturbation/combination
novelty, and $C$ for biological and experimental context. The main calibrator
is
\begin{equation}
 p_i = \sigma\!\left(\exp(a_\phi(z_i))\operatorname{logit}(p_{0,i}) + b_\phi(z_i)\right),
 \label{eq:ptl}
\end{equation}
where $a_\phi$ and $b_\phi$ are small learned heads initialized at zero. The
initial mapping is therefore the identity, and the positive slope preserves
the ordering in $p_0$ for a fixed context. We regularize the two heads toward
zero so that context changes the baseline only when supported by the training
fold. Tree-based and linear heads are retained as diagnostic comparators in
the pilot, while the constrained form in Eq.~\ref{eq:ptl} defines the core
method.

\section{Benchmark and audit design}
\subsection{Stress-aware replay}
The current PTL replay contains 23,226 prediction rows and 6,884
signature-level biological instances across Norman K562, Replogle K562
essential and Replogle RPE1. It uses five grouped folds, three split families
(random, unseen perturbation and dataset held out), and paired
environment-then-instance bootstrap resampling. A three-member aligned seed
surface supplies ensemble-derived uncertainty descriptors for the simple and
linear predictors. The replay is deliberately separated from the official
\gears{} adapter pilot, which uses condition-disjoint splits, a fixed 4,096-gene
panel and native log-variance uncertainty.

The pilot predictor families are global mean, perturbation-matching mean and
Ridge. The reliability heads include raw normalized uncertainty, scalar
calibrators, support/novelty-only features, \ptl{} variants, and tree-based
diagnostics. Identity-rich full-ID variants are reported only as diagnostic
upper bounds. The planned final roster adds a strong linear baseline and
compatible CPA, scGPT and PRESCRIBE implementations; those learned-model
runs are not silently substituted by the present pilot.

\subsection{Semantic and coverage audit}
Before adding contexts, we inspect raw H5AD metadata without loading expression
matrices. The Replogle K562 GWPS file contains 1,989,578 cells and 9,866
non-control perturbation labels. Cell-level label parsing and QC retain nearly
all cells, but the batch--perturbation minimum-cell rule retains 77,168 cells,
only 31 non-control labels and 440 reference groups. We therefore keep GWPS
for the \gears{} contract pilot and use K562 essential for the current common
matrix rather than presenting sparse GWPS coverage as broad benchmark evidence.

The same audit corrects biological semantics before model comparison. Tian
CRISPRa and CRISPRi are iPSC-induced-neuron screens, not K562 environments.
Frangieh RNA is a melanoma co-culture screen whose raw `perturbation\_2' field
contains Control, Co-culture and IFN$\gamma$ conditions, not a THP-1 label. The
staged preprocessing retains these fields and generates 737 signatures across
the three conditions (245, 245 and 247, respectively) from 216,931 retained
cells.

\section{Results}
\subsection{Native uncertainty is not transportability-invariant}
Figure~\ref{fig:native-uq} shows the central failure mode motivating \ptl{}.
Uncertainty quantiles occupy different ranges across split families, and the
relationship between the quantile and realized delta-cosine fidelity changes
across global mean, matching mean and Ridge predictors. In the dataset-held-
out replay, mean fidelity is 0.176 for the global mean, 0.167 for the matching
mean and 0.088 for Ridge; the corresponding values in the random split are
0.226, 0.376 and 0.431. The numbers should be read as a stress diagnostic,
not as a predictor ranking: the held-out environments and the predictor
families have different support conditions.

\subsection{Target-free PTL improves selective decisions in the pilot}
Table~\ref{tab:pilot} reports the environment-macro replay. Raw normalized UQ
has AURC 0.3790, while the current PTL random-forest head has AURC 0.3480.
The same comparison reduces FTR at 80\% retained coverage from 0.5500 to
0.5025 and Brier score from 0.3644 to 0.1754. The paired hierarchical
bootstrap intervals are shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:selective}; they resample
environments first and biological instances second.

\subsection{Reliability uses multiple deployment-time information blocks}
The feature audit identifies 51 deployment features in the full pilot head,
including uncertainty, prediction/context geometry, support, novelty and
design-indicator blocks. Removing uncertainty features lowers the FTR gain
versus raw UQ from 0.4758 to 0.4564, while removing perturbation novelty lowers
it to 0.4709. Removing context-distance features has little effect on this
small replay (gain 0.4770), which is evidence about the present surface rather
than evidence that biological context is uninformative in general.

The intended expansion is an incremental information ladder
$U\rightarrow U+P\rightarrow U+P+S\rightarrow U+P+S+N\rightarrow
U+P+S+N+C$. The current table does not contain all five incremental fits, so
we expose the ladder as a preregistered next-stage comparison rather than
interpolating missing results. Figure~\ref{fig:blocks} displays the actual
feature counts, current block-removal probes and the deployment-only rule.

\section{Discussion}
The pilot supports a methodological claim rather than a universal performance
claim. A predictor's uncertainty is an input to reliability assessment, not a
guarantee that its ordering remains valid after a shift in perturbation,
support or biological context. On the present replay, target-free deployment
features can improve selective decisions, and the improvement is measurable
with environment-first aggregation rather than a pooled row average.

The data audit is part of the scientific result. A raw screen with almost two
million cells can become a narrow perturbation surface when perturbations are
distributed across batches and a batch-aware minimum-cell rule is applied.
Treating that surface as broad coverage would conflate cell count with
transportability. The semantic audit similarly prevents a model from receiving
incorrect context labels: the Tian and Frangieh fields describe different
biological and experimental axes, and those axes should enter the registry
explicitly.

The next phase is to complete staged Tian/Frangieh signature validation, audit
the Xu mapping and Adamson full candidate, and run the formal predictor roster
on the common K562-essential/RPE1/Norman surface plus context expansion. Only
after those checks will the incremental information ladder, native learned
model UQ and final multi-seed claims be promoted into the headline results.

\section{Limitations and reproducibility}
The present evidence is a CPU one-epoch \gears{} adapter pilot plus a
five-fold PTL replay of existing predictor outputs. It validates contracts,
data separation, split integrity and the selective estimand; it does not
establish the final generalization ranking of GEARS, CPA, scGPT or PRESCRIBE.
The raw GWPS surface is explicitly coverage-limited under the current
batch-aware signature contract. The learned-model roster and full multi-
environment context comparison remain pending, even though the Tian and
Frangieh preprocessing surfaces are now staged. These limitations are visible in the registry and the audit
manifests rather than hidden behind a single benchmark score.
