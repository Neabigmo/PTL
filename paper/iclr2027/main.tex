\begin{abstract}
Single-cell perturbation predictors are increasingly deployed across cell
states, perturbation modalities and experimental conditions, yet a predictor's
confidence need not retain the same meaning after such a shift. We study
whether reliability can itself be learned from information available at
prediction time. We introduce \ptl{}, an identity-free reliability layer that
combines predictor uncertainty with prediction geometry, training support,
perturbation novelty and known biological context. A formal benchmark contains
1,881 condition-level biological instances from eight canonical environments,
three verified predictor families (matching mean, an Ahlmann--Eltze-style
bilinear model and \slim{} with a public STRING embedding), three shared model
seeds, and a frozen 60/20/20 perturbation split. The deployment feature surface
is constructed from training-side and design-side information only. Relative
to an equal-capacity U-only random-forest baseline, \ptl{} reduces
environment-macro AURC from 0.642 to 0.614 in-domain, from 0.656 to 0.641
when environments are held out, and from 0.663 to 0.638 when predictor
families are held out. The transfer atlas shows that these gains are not
uniform: reliability knowledge is useful for some source--target pairs and
harmful for others. Our result is therefore not that confidence is invariant,
but that prediction failure contains a structured component that can be
reused under explicit biological shifts.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Single-cell perturbation models convert large screens into predictions of
cellular responses. Their usefulness, however, depends on whether a user can
decide which predictions to trust before the corresponding experiment is
performed. Existing predictors span simple matching baselines, linear
perturbation models, generative models and graph neural networks such as
\gears{}~\citep{norman2019manifolds,lotfollahi2019scgen,bunne2023cellot,
lotfollahi2023cpa,roohani2023gears}. Most predictors expose some uncertainty
descriptor, but uncertainty is not automatically comparable across a K562
screen, an RPE1 screen, an iPSC-neuron screen or a melanoma co-culture.

This paper asks a narrower operational question: can a target-free reliability
layer improve selective prediction under structured biological shift? The
layer is not a new response predictor. It ranks predictions by their expected
failure risk using only quantities available at prediction time, while the
observed response is used only to form calibration labels and final test
outcomes. This separation matters because experimental support and assay
reproducibility can themselves be correlated with the observed target.

Our central claim is deliberately conditional:
\begin{quote}
\emph{Confidence changes meaning across biology, but reliability transfer is
structured, measurable and partly reusable.}
\end{quote}

We make four contributions. First, we define a formal, deployment-safe
reliability surface with canonical environment and biological-instance keys.
Second, we compare \ptl{} with an equal-capacity U-only random forest and an
incremental information ladder, so gains cannot be attributed only to adding a
more flexible classifier. Third, we build a source-to-target reliability
atlas that makes transfer heterogeneity visible rather than averaging it
away. Fourth, we release machine-readable contracts, formal-v2 source tables,
vector figures and reproducible checks that keep unavailable predictors out of
scientific claims.

\section{Problem formulation}
For biological instance $i$, let $\hat y_i$ be a predictor output and $y_i$
the observed delta-expression response. We compute a fidelity $F_i$ from the
prediction and response and define continuous risk $R_i=1-F_i$. A binary
reliability label is used only for calibration diagnostics:
\begin{equation}
 T_i = \mathbb{I}\left[F_i \geq \tau\,\operatorname{median}
 (F_{\mathrm{validation}})\right], \qquad \tau=0.8.
\end{equation}
The threshold is computed separately from validation perturbations for each
predictor--environment surface. Transfer results use continuous realized risk
and AURC/eAURC as the primary outcomes, avoiding a target-dependent threshold
in a held-out environment.

For a coverage level $c$, selective risk is the mean $R_i$ among the highest
confidence-scored $c$ fraction. AURC is the integral of this curve; lower is
better. We report environment-first averages, paired hierarchical bootstrap
intervals, risk at 50/80\% coverage, false-transportability rate (FTR), Brier
score, AUROC and AUPRC as secondary diagnostics.

\section{Deployment-safe reliability layer}
\subsection{Feature contract}
The formal benchmark keeps four evidence surfaces separate: predictions,
deployment features, observed outcomes and split assignments. The headline
feature vector contains five blocks: $U$ (five predictor-uncertainty
features), $P$ (four prediction-geometry features), $S$ (three training-
support features), $N$ (four novelty features), and $C$ (five known-context
features).
The $S$ block is computed from training perturbations only: exact and
component support, nearest training geometry and local training density. The
$C$ block contains cell context, perturbation modality, readout modality,
experimental condition and platform. Realized target cell counts, reference
group membership, QC outcomes, target-side reproducibility, fidelity and all
environment/predictor identifiers are excluded from the headline matrix.

\subsection{Calibration}
We normalize each uncertainty descriptor using calibration rows only. For a
deployment feature vector $z_i$, \ptl{} starts from the scalar UQ confidence
$p_{0,i}$ and applies a context-conditioned correction,
\begin{equation}
 p_i = \sigma\!\left(\exp(a_\phi(z_i))\operatorname{logit}(p_{0,i})+b_\phi(z_i)\right),
 \label{eq:ptl}
\end{equation}
where the heads are initialized at zero and regularized toward zero. The
headline implementation is an identity-free random forest using the same
capacity for the U-only comparator and the full $U+P+S+N+C$ surface. We also
fit the incremental ladder
\[
 U \rightarrow U+P \rightarrow U+P+S \rightarrow U+P+S+N
 \rightarrow U+P+S+N+C.
\]

\section{Formal benchmark}
The benchmark contains 1,881 biological instances, each defined by
environment, perturbation condition, dose and time point. Batch-level
signatures are aggregated into a weighted condition-level delta rather than
being counted as independent samples. The eight canonical environments cover
Norman K562 CRISPRa, Replogle K562 essential and RPE1 CRISPRi, Tian iPSC-neuron
CRISPRa and CRISPRi, and three Frangieh patient-derived melanoma CRISPRn
conditions (control, co-culture and IFN$\gamma$). The common scoring panel has
6,897 genes; model fitting retains each predictor's native gene space.

All predictor families use the same frozen split seed 20260907 and the same
model seeds $0,1,2$. The split is 60\% predictor training, 20\% reliability
calibration and 20\% untouched final test. The verified roster comprises
matching mean, the bilinear Ahlmann--Eltze reproduction with $K=10$ and
$\lambda=0.1$, and \slim{}, a lightweight STRING-embedding predictor. GEARS
has a formal adapter path but remains ready-not-executed in this workspace;
CPA, scGPT and PRESCRIBE remain dependency-missing or optional. These rows
are status metadata and never enter averages, plots or prose as scores.

\section{Results}
\subsection{Confidence semantics vary across biology}
Figure~\ref{fig:confidence} shows the motivating phenomenon on formal test
rows. Within the same nominal confidence range, realized risk shifts across
K562, RPE1, iPSC-neuron and melanoma contexts. The raw-UQ heatmap also shows
that discrimination is not a property of the uncertainty descriptor alone;
it depends on the predictor and environment in which that descriptor is
interpreted.

\subsection{Deployment-safe PTL improves selective prediction}
Table~\ref{tab:formal} compares the full PTL surface with the fair U-only
random-forest baseline. The full model improves the environment-macro AURC in
all three protocols in the current formal surface. Paired bootstrap intervals
for the full PTL versus raw normalized UQ comparison are reported in the
machine-readable manifest; the implementation also records every information
ablation under the same validation-to-test protocol.

\subsection{Reliability knowledge transfers unevenly but systematically}
The source-to-target atlas in Fig.~\ref{fig:atlas} trains PTL on one source
environment's validation rows and evaluates it on each target environment's
untouched test rows. It therefore exposes transfer topology rather than
collapsing all held-out environments into one number. Positive and negative
cells coexist, and target-level summaries differ substantially. We interpret
the relationship between transfer gain and shared context, modality,
platform, condition and prediction geometry descriptively; the atlas is not a
causal analysis.

\subsection{Information blocks and robustness controls}
Figure~\ref{fig:robustness} reports the complete formal information ladder.
The primary comparison is PTL versus U-only RF, while scalar calibration
variants remain diagnostics. The same PTL score is reused for risk, FTR and
Brier summaries, so the result is not obtained by retraining a separate
reliability model for each metric. Restricting to reliability-positive test
outcomes does not change the direction of the selective comparison in the
current formal surface.

\section{Discussion}
The formal results support a calibrated scientific statement rather than a
universal transfer claim. Predictor confidence changes meaning across
biological environments, and a target-free reliability layer can extract
additional signal from prediction geometry, training support, novelty and
known context. The transfer atlas then shows where that knowledge is reusable
and where it is not. This is a more informative object than a single pooled
improvement because a negative source--target cell is itself evidence about
the boundary of transportability.

The distinction between deployment evidence and outcome evidence is central.
Cell counts, QC results and perturbation reproducibility are valuable for
auditing the benchmark, but they are not available when predicting an unseen
perturbation and therefore cannot be headline PTL inputs. Similarly,
experimental reproducibility and alternate response metrics are controls on
the outcome surface, not shortcuts into the feature matrix.

\section{Limitations and reproducibility}
The current scientific roster contains three verified predictor families;
dependency-heavy CPA and scGPT runs, as well as the formal GEARS execution,
are not presented as completed scores. The Ahlmann--Eltze implementation is
guarded by a small oracle comparison and the \slim{} embedding is retained as
a local public reference artifact. The atlas is descriptive and does not
identify a causal mechanism for transfer. Finally, the current figures report
the formal-v2 results available in this workspace; experimental split-half
reproducibility and independent Systema/scPertEval metrics remain planned
controls and must be added before making stronger assay-robustness claims.
