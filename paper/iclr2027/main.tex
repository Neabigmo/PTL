\begin{abstract}
Confidence scores from single-cell perturbation predictors are often treated
as if they preserve the same ordering of easy and hard perturbations across
biological settings. We separate this question from a confounding source:
whether each target condition was refit. In a controlled Frangieh ladder, a
source-context predictor is fit once per shared perturbation-label fold and
scored unchanged against three target outcomes. Across three fidelity
definitions, matched perturbations show source-frozen rank displacement, with
pairwise inversion rates ranging from $0.10$ to $0.32$ across source contexts.
Their response programs also shift strongly (mean $1-\cos$ distance
$=0.79$--$0.85$), but that response displacement is descriptive and does not
imply a causal mechanism. A matched raw-cell audit uses 30 independent
half-splits and pair-specific cell budgets. The resulting cross-context rank
displacement is not consistently above the joint measurement-plus-model noise
floor: the macro mean $\Delta_{\mathrm{joint}}$ is $-0.009$ for delta cosine,
$+0.006$ for Systema centroid accuracy and $-0.036$ for absolute-effect rank
agreement, with bootstrap intervals generally crossing zero. The previously
reported context-adapted OOF inversion is therefore retained as secondary
deployment evidence, not as a frozen-predictor causal diagnostic. A train-only
monotonic Platt recalibration changes probability calibration while producing
zero AURC change and zero strict-order disagreements across 45
environment--predictor groups.
The broad formal benchmark contains 1,603 condition-level biological instances
from eight semantically verified environments, three completed predictor
families, an 8,229-gene shared evaluation panel, and a frozen 60/20/20
biological-instance split. PTL is evaluated later as a deployment-safe probe;
its transport evidence remains heterogeneous rather than being used to
overstate portability.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Single-cell perturbation models turn large screens into predictions of
cellular responses and thereby offer a way to prioritize experiments over a
combinatorial intervention space. Their practical value is consequently not
only the average fidelity of a screen, but whether a researcher can decide
which predictions to trust before spending experimental budget. Predictors
span matching baselines, linear perturbation models, generative models and
graph neural networks such as \gears{}~\citep{norman2019manifolds,lotfollahi2019scgen,bunne2023cellot,lotfollahi2023cpa,roohani2023gears}.
Each model may expose an uncertainty score, but a score learned in a K562
screen need not have the same meaning in RPE1, iPSC-derived neurons or
patient-derived melanoma.

Accuracy alone does not settle this decision problem. Recent analyses show
that deep perturbation predictors do not yet reliably outperform simple
linear baselines~\citep{ahlmann2025simple,wong2025simple}, while Systema
shows that control-referenced metrics can be inflated by systematic variation
and that perturbation-specific evaluation is substantially harder
\citep{systema2025,scperteval2026}. These results make the ordering of
trusted versus risky predictions an object of interest in its own right, not
an afterthought to a single benchmark score.

Uncertainty, calibration and selective-prediction work provides tools for
turning scores into decisions, including calibration maps, abstention rules
and distribution-shift audits~\citep{guo2017calibration,geifman2017selective,ovadia2019uncertainty,liang2024selective}.
However, these tools usually ask whether a score tracks error in one task or
whether a representation transports across domains; they do not establish
that the identity of easy and hard perturbations survives a biological change
\citep{jalaldoust2024transport}. We therefore study the narrower operational
question of whether reliability ordering can be transported to a new setting
without observing target outcomes.

Our central object is reliability reordering:
\begin{quote}
\emph{Biological context can rewrite which matched perturbations are easy or
hard, but a valid claim must separate source-frozen prediction from
context-adapted refitting and must exceed a matched measurement-plus-model
noise floor. We diagnose this as reliability reordering, separate
response-program displacement from outcome-derived risk, and test whether
monotonic recalibration can repair the ordering.}
\end{quote}
We use \emph{Reliability Shift} only as an umbrella term for the broader
difficulty, ranking and calibration audits.

We make four contributions. First, we turn Frangieh into a Claim Lock ladder
with a source-frozen primary estimand, a separately labelled context-adapted
OOF secondary estimand, and a matched raw-cell measurement/joint noise audit.
Second, we add an orthogonal
CRISPRko/CRISPRi extension as an evaluation-only source-response persistence
baseline, explicitly separating it from trained Frangieh predictors. Third,
we show with a monotonic recalibration counterfactual that probability
calibration cannot repair a strict reliability ordering. Fourth, we compare
PTL with an equal-capacity U-only probe, audit prospective source selection
and modern-model validity, and retain negative transport evidence rather than
tuning it away.

\section{Biological context reorders prediction reliability}
For biological instance $i$, let $\hat y_i$ be a predictor output and $y_i$
the observed delta-expression response. Fidelity $F_i$ is computed from the
prediction and response, and continuous risk is $R_i=1-F_i$. A binary label is
used only to calibrate selective diagnostics,
\begin{equation}
 T_i = \mathbb{I}\left[F_i \geq \tau\,\operatorname{median}
 (F_{\mathrm{validation}})\right], \qquad \tau=0.8.
\end{equation}
The threshold is fit on validation perturbations separately for each
predictor--environment surface. Final transfer analyses use continuous risk
and AURC, so a target environment is not tuned with its own outcomes.

For coverage $c$, selective risk is the mean risk among the highest-scored
$c$ fraction. AURC is the integral of this curve; lower is better. We report
environment-first averages, paired hierarchical bootstrap intervals, risk at
50/80\% coverage, false-trust rate, Brier score, AUROC and AUPRC.

\subsection{Same confidence, different realized risk}
We fit predictor-relative confidence bins on calibration-side scores and apply
those fixed bins to untouched test rows. Within each bin we report the global
risk, the between-environment risk variance, the maximum environment gap and a
weighted absolute deviation of environment means from the global bin mean,
which we call \emph{contextual grouping loss}. Matched-confidence pairs are
formed from the bin assignment alone; their outcome differences are measured
  only afterward. A hierarchical environment--biological-instance bootstrap
  provides uncertainty. This makes the title's phenomenon quantitative without
  turning measured target outcomes into deployment features.
  The corrected grouping-loss analysis aggregates within-bin quantities rather
  than recomputing them on the unconditioned test frame. It reports conditional
  contextual grouping loss, matched-confidence same- and cross-environment
  risk gaps, their difference, and a confidence-bin-weighted maximum gap, with
  hierarchical bootstrap intervals. These statistics quantify residual
  heterogeneity after confidence conditioning without treating the statistic as
  a new generic calibration concept.

\subsection{Controlled Shift Ladder: difficulty and ranking components}
The controlled Frangieh ladder provides the paper's Claim Lock diagnostic. For
each condition pair we retain only the common perturbation labels and keep the
source predictor fixed while changing the target outcome. The shared five-fold
assignment contains 243 labels. Across the three source contexts and three
fidelity definitions, source-frozen pairwise risk inversions range from
$0.10$ to $0.32$ and normalized rank displacement ranges from $0.073$ to
$0.229$. These are direct evidence that a reliability ordering can change
under a biological condition change, but they are not by themselves evidence
that the change exceeds measurement noise. The same matched labels have
response-program shifts of $0.79$--$0.85$ in mean $1-\cos$ distance. The
direct, label-level response-shift/rank-displacement association is weak
($\rho=0.04$--$0.17$), with perturbation-bootstrap intervals crossing zero in
two of the three condition pairs. We therefore use response displacement to
diagnose that the biological object has moved, not to claim that it explains a
particular reliability reversal.
For a pair of environments, the primary rank estimand is
\begin{equation}
 D_{\mathrm{rank}} = \frac{\#\{(p,q):\operatorname{sign}(R_{p,e_1}-R_{q,e_1})
 \ne \operatorname{sign}(R_{p,e_2}-R_{q,e_2})\}}
 {\#\{(p,q):\text{both risk differences are strict}\}},
 \end{equation}
and we also report the normalized perturbation rank displacement
$d_p=|\operatorname{rank}_{e_1}(R_p)-\operatorname{rank}_{e_2}(R_p)|/(n-1)$.
Ties are excluded from the strict denominator rather than assigned an
arbitrary direction.
The context-adapted five-fold OOF surface expands the matched condition sets to
243--246 labels and gives $0.248$--$0.368$ strict pairwise inversion rates;
these are retained as secondary deployment evidence because each condition has
its own fitted predictor. The source-frozen primary is accompanied by a raw-cell
measurement lock: QC-passed cells are independently split 30 times, controls
are split independently, and each condition pair uses
$\lfloor\min(n_{p,e_1},n_{p,e_2})/2\rfloor$ cells per half. Across the three
source contexts and three metrics, matched-budget cross-context displacement
does not clear the joint measurement-plus-model floor. The macro mean
$\Delta_{\mathrm{joint}}$ is $-0.009$ for delta cosine, $+0.006$ for Systema
centroid accuracy and $-0.036$ for absolute-effect rank agreement; the
2,000-draw paired intervals generally cross zero. Thus the data support
context-dependent reordering as an observed phenomenon, while not supporting a
stable excess beyond the measured noise floor. Confidence tracking on the
context-adapted surface is $0.122$--$0.152$, below the independent
confidence-permutation null of $0.249$--$0.251$. The two surfaces are reported
separately and are not pooled.

The decomposition makes the base-risk alternative explicit. Difficulty shift is
the variation of $\mu_e=\mathbb{E}[R\mid E=e]$ across environments. Ranking
shift is measured within each environment by Spearman and Kendall concordance
between confidence and $-R$, together with environment-normalized selective
AURC. Semantic/calibration shift is tested, but not presumed, by comparing the held-out error of
$R=\alpha_e+h(c)$ with $R=\alpha_e+h_e(c)$, where confidence bins are defined
on calibration rows and biological instances are the held-out units. We report
the interaction improvement, hierarchical interval and an environment-wise
permutation null. In the present data, difficulty shift and ranking
heterogeneity are established, whereas the semantic interaction is not
consistently supported; the claim is therefore limited to the identifiable
difficulty and ranking components.

The controlled Frangieh comparison uses shared perturbations across control,
co-culture and IFN$\gamma$. The primary mean-matching surface has degenerate
confidence for the Frangieh method panels, so its ranking entries are not
counted as support. The matched Frangieh surfaces provide a microscope for
condition changes within study, complementing the eight-environment telescope;
coverage-limited axes remain explicitly marked in the machine-readable audit.

\subsection{Response reprogramming across modality, time and cell context}
The Frangieh OOF surface supplies a dense within-study condition ladder and is
the primary trained-predictor evidence, while the matched head-to-head
resource~\citep{headtohead2026crispr} supplies orthogonal axes with exact target
intersections. We ingest guide-level SCEPTRE effects only for an
evaluation-only source-response persistence baseline: a source target mean is
persisted to destination guide outcomes, not pooled with the formal Frangieh
predictor benchmark and not presented as a newly trained perturbation model.
Guide modality is taken from the released reference table; ambiguous
``either'' guides and negative controls are excluded. Day-14 K562 is compared
separately for CRISPR knockout and CRISPR interference, while Day 7/10 and
K562/A549 comparisons are stratified by the same modality. This design keeps
the perturbation intersection fixed within every comparison and treats the
response-program distance $G_p=1-\cos(\Delta y_{p,e_1},\Delta y_{p,e_2})$ as
descriptive. In particular, a large $G_p$ is evidence that the measured
response object changed, not evidence that it caused a reliability reversal.
Across the six modality-stratified external comparisons, exact target
intersections contain 22--25 targets, risk-order inversions are 12.0--36.4\%,
and mean response-program shifts are $0.66$--$0.97$. The persistence baseline
has zero confidence-tracking rate for these inverted pairs, which is a
diagnostic failure of this source-only confidence signal rather than evidence
that every perturbation model is uncalibratable.

\section{Recalibration cannot repair reordered reliability}
The recalibration counterfactual makes the distinction operational. The
calibration-side scalar score is mapped by a train-only monotonic Platt model
and evaluated on untouched test rows. The map changes Brier score in some
groups, but it preserves every comparable pairwise order: all 45 groups have
zero order disagreements and zero AURC change. Isotonic regression is reported
separately because its non-decreasing map can introduce ties. Formally, if
$g$ is strictly increasing, then $C_i<C_j$ if and only if
$g(C_i)<g(C_j)$, so strict order is preserved exactly. If $g$ is only
non-decreasing, $C_i<C_j$ implies $g(C_i)\leq g(C_j)$: ties may be introduced,
but an existing strict order cannot be reversed. The fitted Platt map is
$g(C)=\sigma(aC+b)$; our implementation asserts $a>0$ before using it as a
monotonic calibration contract. This rules out probability-scale recalibration
as an explanation for the observed strict ordering change, while leaving open
richer non-monotonic predictors.

\section{Deployment consequence: prospective reliability transport is difficult}
Let $d_{st}$ denote outcome-free descriptors between source environment $s$
and target environment $t$. The evaluated descriptor vector contains exactly
the frozen fields: same cell context, modality, platform, condition, dataset
family, context+modality, geometry distance and uncertainty-distribution
distance. These descriptors are measured before target outcomes are available;
we do not claim that they identify all useful biological similarity.

The transport problem is source selection, not response prediction. We fit a
small gain model on source--target pairs for which outcomes are available in
the training portion and evaluate it with strict leave-target-environment-out
cross-validation. Target outcomes are never used to build $d_{st}$ or select a
source during the held-out evaluation. Performance is assessed by gain-rank
correlation, positive-transfer discrimination, source-selection regret and
calibration against a permutation null.

\begin{equation}
 r_s(x)=f_{\theta}(U,P,S,N,C), \qquad
 r_U(x)=f_{\theta}(U),
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
 g(s,t)=h_{\psi}(d_{st}), \qquad
 \operatorname{prospective\_gate}(s,t)=
 \begin{cases}
 r_s, & \widehat g(s,t)>0 \text{ and } \Pr(g>0\mid d_{st})\geq\eta,\\
 r_U, & \text{otherwise.}
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
We call this a prospective gating baseline rather than a safeguard: it is an
auditable deployment heuristic, not a demonstrated protective method. Its
fallback is deliberately asymmetric, returning U-only when the fixed
validation-only acceptance criterion is not met.

\section{Deployment-safe PTL probe}
\subsection{Deployment-safe feature surface}
The benchmark keeps predictions, deployment features, observed outcomes and
split assignments separate. The headline feature vector contains five blocks:
$U$ (five uncertainty features), $P$ (four prediction-geometry features), $S$
(three training-support features), $N$ (four novelty features) and $C$ (five
declared context features). All normalizers, nearest-neighbor transforms and
categorical encoders are fit on calibration-side rows only. Environment and
predictor identifiers, fidelity, continuous risk, realized cell counts,
reference membership, QC outcomes and target reproducibility are excluded
from the headline feature matrix.

\subsection{Fair baseline and prospective gating baseline}
The headline PTL estimator is a random forest over $U+P+S+N+C$; the U-only
baseline has the same estimator capacity and uses only $U$. The information
ladder adds blocks in the order
$U\rightarrow U+P\rightarrow U+P+S\rightarrow U+P+S+N\rightarrow
U+P+S+N+C$. Prospective source selection is evaluated separately from the
row-level reliability score and falls back exactly to U-only when its fixed
validation-only rule is not met.

\section{Generality and robustness}
\subsection{Benchmark and protocol}
The benchmark registry contains 1,603 condition-level biological instances
across Norman K562, Replogle RPE1, Tian iPSC-derived-neuron CRISPRa and CRISPRi,
three Frangieh patient-derived-melanoma conditions, and Xu HEK293 CRISPRi.
Adamson multi-accession and Replogle K562 GWPS surfaces remain supplementary
because their pinned processed surfaces do not meet the headline coverage
contract; QC thresholds and reference matching are not relaxed. The shared
scoring panel contains 8,229 genes, while each predictor retains its native
training gene space.

All completed predictor families use split seed 20260907 and model seeds
$0,1,2$. The protocol is 60\% predictor training, 20\% reliability
calibration and 20\% untouched final test, grouped by biological instance.
The completed roster comprises matching mean, an Ahlmann--Eltze-style
bilinear model with $K=10$ and $\lambda=0.1$, and \slim{} with a public STRING
embedding.

As an external-contract audit rather than a headline comparison, the official
pinned \gears{} stack was run remotely on the Norman K562, Replogle K562 GWPS
and Replogle RPE1 surfaces for seeds $0,1,2$. Each run used 20 epochs, the
official condition-disjoint split, a dataset-frozen 4,096-gene panel, finite
native uncertainty outputs and persisted validation predictions. This
validation-backed audit enters the Reliability Shift evidence bundle for the
two supported environments; Replogle K562 GWPS remains a test-only external
audit and is outside the headline reliability surface.

\subsection{Transport heterogeneity and robustness}
Across 280 source--target observations from five split seeds, the strict
  target-unseen predictor achieved positive-transfer AUROC 0.503, with a
  1,000-permutation mean of 0.456. Ungated negative transfer was 36.4\%. The
prospective gating baseline fell back to U-only in 10\% of 40 target-by-split
decisions; among non-fallback decisions, selected-source negative transfer
was 41.7\%. Mean realized gain versus target-excluded U-only was +0.0012,
with 21 targets helped, 4 unchanged and 15 harmed. Because selected-source
harm is higher than the ungated candidate-pair rate, the baseline is evidence
that this natural heuristic is insufficient, not a protective method. We
therefore report within-target rank concordance, candidate-positive fraction
and empirical permutation p-values as the primary source-selection diagnostics.

Metric robustness is high but not identical: the released robustness bundle
reuses the saved PTL scores and recomputes selective outcomes under the pinned
Systema entrypoint and the declared alternative diagnostics, without
per-metric refitting. The fixed-budget analysis evaluates the same learned
scores at 20, 50 and 80\% budgets without budget-specific retraining. These
audits qualify the Reliability Shift result; they are not additional training
or a pooled superiority claim. Under the pinned Systema centroid-AURC
entrypoint, PTL is $0.44381$ versus $0.44279$ for U-only, with the same mean
risk ($0.471253$); this near-tie is reported as robustness evidence, not a
method win.

The Claim Lock separates this broad robustness bundle from the controlled
Frangieh diagnostic. The source-frozen artifact stores the complete prediction
vectors for the three source contexts, the shared five-fold assignment, the
three model members and the 8,229-gene checksum. The raw-cell lock then uses
the same vectors with independent half-A/B outcomes, matched cell budgets and
30 split seeds. The primary confirmatory deltas compare cross-context rank
displacement with the larger of the two target-context floors. None of the
three metrics has a consistently positive macro delta over the joint floor.
This is the appropriate boundary for the present evidence: context-dependent
reordering is observable, but its excess over measurement plus model noise is
not established. No valid independent same-predictor matched-context source
was available; Tian has only one shared label and the head-to-head resource is
an evaluation-only persistence baseline, so we do not convert either into a
replication claim.

The five-seed stability atlas is used to characterize, rather than tune,
transport. Across the same 56 off-diagonal pairs per baseline, the mean
transfer gain was $+0.0003$ for raw scalar UQ and $+0.0055$ for the equal-capacity
U-only baseline; negative transfer occurred in 40.7\% and 36.4\% of the
corresponding observations. A separate raw-cell split-half audit on Norman and
RPE1 yielded 245 paired perturbations with median split-half cosine 0.814; 153
of these pairs joined to the reliability surface for the risk-stratified audit.
On the primary Norman mean-matching/PTL slice, low- versus high-reproducibility
strata had mean risks $0.469$ versus $0.145$, within-stratum normalized AURCs
$0.967$ versus $0.824$, and a corrected fixed-calibration-bin grouping loss of
$0.085$ across the low/middle/high strata.  The reproducibility-stratum
interaction cross-fit improved over the additive stratum-plus-bin null by
$-0.007$, so this audit does not support a positive semantic-shift claim.
Replogle/RPE1 contributed only four joined rows (three low and one middle),
with no high-reproducibility stratum; it is therefore reported as coverage
limited rather than used for a high-versus-low inference.  Reproducibility is
used only for this evaluation/audit stratification and is not entered into
PTL.  These are stability summaries, not additional tuning or a new
superiority claim.

\section{Discussion}
The experiments support a limitation-centered view of reliability. Reliability
Shift is visible as changes in environment difficulty and within-environment
ranking, while the Claim Lock shows that the observed controlled reordering is
not yet a noise-excess result; the environment-dependent semantic component is
reported only to the extent supported by its null test. PTL's additional deployment-side descriptors
improve ranking in familiar settings and under predictor-family holdout, yet
environment holdout does not produce a statistically established improvement.
The prospective gating baseline makes fallback behavior explicit, but its
selected-source harm rate is not lower than the ungated candidate-pair rate; it
is therefore not a protective safeguard.

Context is allowed deployment information, but it changes what transportability
means; all headline PTL features remain target-outcome-free and explicit-ID-free.

\section{Related work and positioning}
The first dispute is evaluative. Ahlmann--Eltze et al. show that deep
perturbation predictors do not yet outperform simple linear baselines, whereas
Systema argues that control-referenced scores can reward systematic variation
and proposes perturbation-specific references~\citep{ahlmann2025simple,systema2025}.
The scPertEval framework likewise emphasizes that metric choice, split design
and reference construction are part of the scientific question
\citep{scperteval2026}; independent work finds that simple controls can exceed
deep models~\citep{wong2025simple}. These studies tell us how a model ranks on
an evaluation surface, but do not ask whether the same perturbations remain
easy or hard after a matched biological shift. We add that missing paired
ordering question rather than claim a new leaderboard.

The second line of work concerns biological context and generalization.
Generalization benchmarks and context-aware virtual-cell proposals emphasize
cellular context as a source of out-of-distribution variation
\citep{generalizable2025benchmark,dibaeinia2026context,molina2026decomposition}.
Frangieh et al. provide a patient-model, multimodal condition surface, while
the matched CRISPRko/CRISPRi study supplies orthogonal modality, time and cell
contexts~\citep{frangieh2021multimodal,headtohead2026crispr}. Those works
measure response or prediction changes, but a response shift is not itself a
reliability shift, and neither establishes universal reordering for all
trained predictors. Our primary Frangieh OOF ladder tests the trained-model
ordering directly; the external ladder is deliberately limited to an
evaluation-only persistence baseline.

The third line concerns uncertainty, calibration, selective prediction and
transport. Calibration maps~\citep{guo2017calibration}, selective rules
\citep{geifman2017selective,liang2024selective}, shift audits
\citep{ovadia2019uncertainty}, grouping-loss analysis
\citep{perezlebel2023grouping} and epistemic decision criteria
\citep{melo2026epistemic} improve how scores are interpreted. Recent biological
methods such as PRESCRIBE and the PertEMA reliability layer also target
uncertainty-aware decisions~\citep{prescribe2025,shrestha2026pertema}.
Our object is different: transport of a reliability ordering across biological
contexts. We therefore test what monotonic recalibration cannot change, audit
outcome-free source selection with explicit transport descriptors, and retain
negative transfer instead of presenting PTL as a new uncertainty estimator.

\section{Limitations and reproducibility}
The present claim is bounded by the eight-environment headline surface and the
predictor families for which a common validation/test contract is available.
The source-frozen Frangieh Claim Lock is a primary within-study diagnostic,
not an independent replication: no legal matched source-predictor outcome
surface was available beyond the stated Tian and head-to-head boundaries.
The official \gears{} execution is separately audited with a condition-disjoint
split, a dataset-native gene panel and persisted validation predictions for
Norman and RPE1; GWPS remains test-only. The audit is not used to manufacture a
cross-model superiority claim.
We evaluate three canonical predictor families and conduct an independent
official \gears{} audit; broader modern-model coverage remains future work.
The GWPS local surface is
coverage-limited, and its audit explicitly prevents it from being mistaken
for a genome-wide surface. The reference-group audit is supplemented, but not
replaced, by the two-dataset raw-cell split-half audit, so it cannot support a
universal assay-reproducibility claim. Finally, the transfer predictor is near
  chance in the strict five-split audit, the prospective gate retains nonzero regret and
selected negative transfers, and the atlas is descriptive rather than causal.

All primary outputs are regenerated from the fixed split, native predictor
spaces and shared panel. The released bundle contains prediction contracts,
deployment features, paired bootstrap intervals, transfer matrices,
metric-robustness and fixed-budget tables, source-frozen prediction vectors,
matched-budget measurement/joint floors, guide-coverage and replication-boundary
manifests, split-half audit summaries, and editable vector figures with
source-data manifests. Claims are downgraded whenever confidence intervals,
stability or metric robustness do not support them.
