\begin{abstract}
Confidence scores from single-cell perturbation predictors are often used to
rank experiments, implicitly assuming that the same perturbations remain easy
or hard after a biological change. We separate this observed ordering question
from target refitting and from measurement noise. In a controlled Frangieh
ladder, a source-context predictor is fit once per shared perturbation-label
fold and scored unchanged against three target outcomes. The resulting
source-frozen surface shows substantial continuous rank displacement, but rank
displacement is kept secondary to a tie-aware pairwise ordering estimand. We
compare cross-context order disagreement with matched measurement, model-only
and joint floors; finite within-context floors use the leave-one-replicate-out
U-statistic rather than a positively biased plug-in estimate. The primary audit
uses 30 independent full-size nonparametric raw-cell pseudo-replicates, with
the original split-half analysis retained as sensitivity evidence. Across the
nine directed source/target comparisons, measurement-identifiable and
joint-identifiable excesses are reported with synchronized macro bootstrap
intervals, separately for each metric. The result is metric-dependent rather
than a universal reordering claim: in the full-size Frangieh audit, the
measurement-identifiable excess ranges from 0.0361 to 0.1178 and the
joint-identifiable excess ranges from -0.0175 to 0.0702 across the three metrics.
An outcome-blind Nadig HepG2/Jurkat registry
and independent Claim Lock replication reproduce the same identifiability
boundary.
Monotonic recalibration is relegated to a supplementary lemma because it can
change probability scale but not strict order. PTL is retained only as a
deployment-side probe with heterogeneous, non-protective transport evidence.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Single-cell perturbation models turn large screens into predictions that can
prioritize experiments over a combinatorial intervention space. Their value
therefore depends not only on average fidelity, but also on whether a
researcher can decide which predictions to trust before spending experimental
budget. An uncertainty score learned in K562 need not mean the same thing in
RPE1, iPSC-derived neurons or patient-derived melanoma. This question is
related to, but distinct from, response prediction itself.

Accuracy alone does not settle the decision problem. Deep perturbation
predictors do not yet reliably outperform simple linear baselines, while
Systema and scPertEval show that reference construction, split design and
metric choice can change conclusions~\citep{ahlmann2025simple,wong2025simple,systema2025,scperteval2026}.
The ordering of trusted versus risky predictions is therefore an object in its
own right, not an afterthought to one benchmark score.

Calibration and selective prediction ask whether a score tracks error in a
fixed task, while shift audits ask whether representations transport
\citep{guo2017calibration,geifman2017selective,ovadia2019uncertainty,liang2024selective}.
Neither alone establishes that the identity of easy and hard perturbations
survives a biological change~\citep{jalaldoust2024transport}. We study this
narrower operational question without using target outcomes to choose a
predictor, context pair or feature surface.

Our central claim is an identifiability boundary:
\begin{quote}
\emph{Source-frozen predictions can show substantial cross-context rank
displacement. A claim of context-specific excess is identifiable only after
the same ordering statistic is compared with matched experimental and model
noise floors.}
\end{quote}

We make five contributions. First, we formalize a tie-aware ordering
distribution and an exact measurement-corrected divergence, separating it from
the conservative max-floor stress test. Second, we execute a full-size
raw-cell Claim Lock with fixed source-frozen predictions and an independent
15/15-seed held-out stability check. Third, an outcome-blind registry selects
one Nadig HepG2/Jurkat pair before prediction or risk evaluation and the same
audit is run once. Fourth, we bound the practical consequence: prospective
transport is heterogeneous, so PTL is reported as an auditable deployment
probe rather than a universal reliability estimator. We additionally make
measurement depth, directed decision utility, metric transitions and
cross-dataset predictability explicit audit surfaces; unsupported modern-model
extensions remain feasibility blockers.

\section{Estimands and identifiability boundary}
For a perturbation pair $c=(p,q)$, context $e$, and measurement replicate $r$,
let $R_{p,e}^{(r)}$ be continuous risk from a fixed predictor and define
\begin{equation}
 Z_{c,e}^{(r)} = \operatorname{sign}\!\left(R_{p,e}^{(r)}-R_{q,e}^{(r)}\right)
 \in\{-1,0,+1\}.
\end{equation}
Ties are retained explicitly. The context-specific ordering distribution is
\begin{equation}
 \pi_{c,e}=\left[\Pr(Z=-1),\Pr(Z=0),\Pr(Z=+1)\right].
\end{equation}
For two contexts, observed cross-context disagreement and within-context
measurement disagreement are
\begin{equation}
 D_{\mathrm{cross}}=\mathbb{E}_{c}\left[1-\pi_{c,e_1}^{\mathsf T}\pi_{c,e_2}\right],\qquad
 D_{\mathrm{within},e}=\mathbb{E}_{c}\left[1-\|\pi_{c,e}\|_2^2\right].
\end{equation}
Their measurement-corrected ordering divergence is
\begin{equation}
 D_{\mathrm{corr}}=D_{\mathrm{cross}}-
 \frac{D_{\mathrm{within},e_1}+D_{\mathrm{within},e_2}}{2}
 =\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{c}\left\|\pi_{c,e_1}-\pi_{c,e_2}\right\|_2^2\geq0.
 \label{eq:ordering_identity}
\end{equation}
The equality is an identity, not a causal claim. It measures how the two
ordering distributions differ after accounting for their within-context
measurement distributions. It is distinct from the conservative operational
stress test used below,
\begin{equation}
 \Delta_{\mathrm{joint}}=D_{\mathrm{cross}}-
 \max(D_{\mathrm{joint},e_1},D_{\mathrm{joint},e_2}),\qquad
 \max(D_1,D_2)=\frac{D_1+D_2}{2}+\frac{|D_1-D_2|}{2}.
\end{equation}
Consequently, a negative max-floor excess does not contradict a nonzero
distributional difference in Eq.~\ref{eq:ordering_identity}.

For finite replicate data, the primary within-context estimator is the
leave-one-replicate-out U-statistic. If $(n_{-},n_{0},n_{+})$ are the counts of
the three order outcomes over $n$ replicates, then
\begin{equation}
 \widehat D_{\mathrm{within}}^{U}
 =1-\frac{n_{-}(n_{-}-1)+n_{0}(n_{0}-1)+n_{+}(n_{+}-1)}{n(n-1)}.
 \label{eq:u_floor}
\end{equation}
The finite measurement-identifiable excess replaces the two within terms in
Eq.~\ref{eq:ordering_identity} by Eq.~\ref{eq:u_floor}; the plug-in/V identity
is retained only as an implementation diagnostic.

We keep three estimands separate. The measurement-only floor fixes the
predictor and resamples raw-cell outcomes. The model-only floor fixes the
outcome and crosses independent model members. The joint floor crosses both
sources of randomness. The joint surface is empirically crossed and is not
assumed to be the sum of its two components.

For stable ordering, we also report
$p_{c,e}=\Pr_r(Z_{c,e}^{(r)}=+1)$. Fifteen seeds are used for selection and
fifteen disjoint seeds for evaluation; the two halves are swapped. A pair is
called stable only when its 95\% Beta posterior interval for the strict
direction, conditional on non-ties, lies entirely on one side of $0.5$ and at
least eight strict observations support that direction. This procedure is a stability diagnostic, not an additional
selection of the headline metric.

\section{Controlled Frangieh audit}
The Frangieh surface contains 243 shared perturbation labels across control,
co-culture and IFN$\gamma$. For every global five-fold perturbation-label
assignment, the source predictor is fit once and its prediction vector is
held fixed while the target raw-cell outcome changes. The primary cell audit
uses 30 independent seeds. For each perturbation and context, an independent
with-replacement pseudo-replicate contains the same number of cells as the
matched source/target raw-cell budget; controls are resampled independently.
The three metrics are delta cosine, Systema centroid accuracy and
absolute-effect rank agreement. The original disjoint split-half analysis is
retained as a sensitivity audit. No metric-specific refit, sign-based
selection or target-outcome feature is introduced.

\section{Measurement depth and decision boundary}
The full-size Claim Lock is complemented by a prespecified measurement-depth
curve. We resample 10, 20, 40, 80 and 160 QC-passed raw cells per
environment--perturbation pseudoreplicate, together with the matched full-size
depth. The primary curve freezes the shared perturbation universe at the
highest fixed budget and reuses it at every lower budget
(\texttt{matched\_fixed}); a separate \texttt{coverage} curve records the
naturally available universe. Exact
coverage, mode and SHA256 fingerprints are recorded, so a change in the curve
is inspected as a resolution effect rather than being conflated with label loss.

For each depth, the same burden decomposition is applied at the perturbation
level. The global value is the mean of the per-perturbation burdens, providing
an exact audit identity rather than an approximate post hoc explanation. We
define the 90\%-of-full resolution depth as the first fixed budget whose
identifiable divergence lies within ten percent of the full-depth value. The
detectability threshold is the first budget whose 90\% seed-bootstrap lower
interval is above zero; when no budget passes, the threshold is explicitly
unavailable. These rules make a negative result inspectable without changing
the estimand after seeing the sign.

The scientific estimand and the deployment consequence are kept distinct. At
fixed decision budgets $k/n\in\{0.05,0.10,0.20,0.50\}$, we select the lowest
source risk and evaluate it against the target risk. We report oracle overlap,
target regret, normalized regret and boundary inversion. The boundary
inversion $V_k$ is zero exactly when the strict top-$k$ sets agree; with $m$
mis-selected items, the audit checks the finite bound
$V_k\geq m^2/[k(n-k)]$ under strict tie handling. Measurement-floor and joint-
floor excess regret are reported separately, so a low global ordering
disagreement cannot be read as a deployment guarantee. Decision point estimates
are the frozen 30-seed means; their descriptive seed quantiles are kept
separate from the inferential 90\% intervals, which are the $q_{.05}$--$q_{.95}$
quantiles of the canonical 2,000-draw perturbation-label $\times$
measurement-seed bootstrap.

The plug-in/V identity is checked in every ordering row with machine-precision
error; this guards against interpreting a coding or aggregation mismatch as a
biological result. The primary full-size macro values in Table~\ref{tab:claim-lock}
are computed within synchronized draws. We therefore report metric-dependent
evidence, not a universal context-dependent reordering claim. The max-floor
stress test is intentionally conservative: its sign can remain negative when
the ordering distributions differ, especially when the joint floors are
asymmetric.

The held-out stability audit gives a second guard against pairwise overreading.
Across source/pair/metric surfaces, selection-stable fractions are recorded
before evaluation and held-out inversion/evaluable fractions are reported
unchanged in the machine-readable ordering manifest. These quantities are
descriptive of the fixed contract; they are not used to choose a favorable
context pair.

\subsection{Response displacement is not a causal reliability mechanism}
The trained-predictor surface is complemented by a matched head-to-head
resource~\citep{headtohead2026crispr}. That resource is an evaluation-only
source-response persistence baseline, not a trained perturbation predictor and
is not pooled with the Frangieh Claim Lock. Its modality-, time- and
cell-context comparisons keep exact target intersections fixed and show large
response-program shifts, but those shifts do not establish a causal mechanism
or imply that every predictor must reorder.

\section{Outcome-blind Nadig replication}
Before reading any prediction, risk or inversion result, we scanned 33 local
same-study candidate pairs. The frozen registry records study, contexts, exact
shared perturbations, raw and matched-control cell counts, perturbation and
readout modality, library and guide coverage, batch/context provenance,
exclusion reason, eligibility, and the three evaluation flags. Its SHA256 and
commit are recorded in the regenerated registry manifest; the complete table
is Supplementary Table S1. Selection was materialized with outcome-blind
$B=1$, prediction $P=0$ and risk $R=0$ before the Nadig run.

Nadig HepG2--Jurkat is the sole eligible pair with supported independent-context
provenance: it has 2,392 exact shared
perturbations, matched controls, the same dual-sgRNA library family, parallel
screens, day-7 harvest and 10x 3' v3 RNA readout. GEO metadata supports
independent biological context with batch identifiers nested within context.
Replogle K562-essential--RPE1 has 2,055 shared perturbations but is excluded
because day-6 versus day-7 harvest is inseparable from the context comparison.
The Claim Lock uses the same source-frozen strong-linear predictor, three model
members, 30 seeds, 2,000 paired perturbation-bootstrap draws and both
minimum-cell thresholds. The full-size primary, matched-universe split-half
($n=1255$), and $n\geq40$ ($n=345$) results are reported in the canonical
claim table with synchronized macro intervals for the pairwise-order estimand.
This is one bounded replication, not universal transportability.

The Nadig pair is included in the atlas as an independent Tier 1 pair-level
replication; it is not pooled with the Frangieh depth or decision curves.
Modern-model checkout, preprocessing and semantic eligibility evidence are
reported in the supplement and in the feasibility manifest rather than as a
sixth main figure.

\section{Deployment consequence}
PTL is evaluated only as a deployment-side probe. Its feature surface keeps
predictions, outcome-free descriptors, observed outcomes and split assignments
separate. The source-selection model is trained on source--target pairs whose
outcomes are available in the training portion and evaluated with strict
leave-target-environment-out cross-validation. Target outcomes are never used
to construct held-out descriptors or to select a source.

The deployment decision is deliberately asymmetric:
\begin{equation}
 g(s,t)=h_{\psi}(d_{st}),\qquad
 \operatorname{gate}(s,t)=
 \begin{cases}
 r_s,&\widehat g(s,t)>0\text{ and }\Pr(g>0\mid d_{st})\geq\eta,\\
 r_U,&\text{otherwise.}
 \end{cases}
\end{equation}
Here $r_U$ is the uncertainty-only fallback. Across 280 source--target
observations, the strict target-unseen source-ranking AUROC is 0.503 and
ungated negative transfer is 36.4\%. The gate falls back in 10\% of 40
target-by-split decisions; among non-fallback decisions, selected-source
negative transfer is 41.7\%. The gate is accepted only under a fixed
validation-only acceptance criterion. These results make the gate an audit heuristic,
not a demonstrated safeguard.

\section{Perturbation heterogeneity and prospective predictability}
The per-perturbation decomposition makes it possible to ask whether the
transport boundary is concentrated in a small set of perturbations or broadly
distributed. We treat this as a descriptive heterogeneity analysis. A
source-U-only baseline and a low-capacity ridge model are fit with
leave-one-perturbation-label-out folds using only frozen source descriptors,
including prediction geometry and model-member uncertainty. Target risk and
target response are evaluation-only. Held-out Spearman, training-fold-
threshold AUROC and MAE are reported only when the number of independent
labels supports the fit; calibration Brier scores are not reported because
post-hoc target scaling would be invalid.

\section{Benchmark, modern-model scope and robustness}
The broad registry contains 1,603 condition-level biological instances across
Norman K562, Replogle RPE1, Tian iPSC-derived-neuron CRISPRa and CRISPRi,
three Frangieh patient-derived-melanoma conditions and Xu HEK293 CRISPRi. The
shared scoring panel contains 8,229 genes; predictors retain their native
training spaces. The completed Claim Lock roster is matching mean, an
Ahlmann--Eltze-style bilinear model ($K=10$, $\lambda=0.1$), and \slim{} with
a public STRING embedding. All use a fixed 60/20/20 biological-instance split
with model seeds $0,1,2$.

The modern-model audit is intentionally a feasibility gate. The official
\gears{} stack has a CUDA-runnable external contract with persisted validation
outputs, but not a Frangieh or Nadig frozen prediction vector. The official
TxPert checkout and cached K562 checkpoint reproduce its single-cell-line
inference contract on the user-authorized CUDA environment, but they do not
constitute a same-predictor HepG2/Jurkat vector. Its explicit adapter records
this incompatibility and refuses Claim Lock promotion. The official State
checkout and CLI preprocessing path are separately audited, but no
same-contract source-only vector is promoted without verified training and
held-out prediction artifacts. scGPT is installed but its Windows torchtext
ABI fails before model construction. None of these
external surfaces enters the headline Claim Lock; this prevents modern-model
availability, checkpoint success or GPU utilization from being mistaken for a
cross-context scientific result. The shared 1,536-gene source-control panel is
frozen for future same-contract adaptation and is not used to retrofit a
result into the current claim.

\section{Discussion}
The evidence supports a limitation-centered interpretation. Source-frozen
orderings visibly change across matched biological settings.
Eq.~\ref{eq:ordering_identity} provides the population nonnegative
distributional measure, while Eq.~\ref{eq:u_floor} provides the finite-sample
estimator used for headline inference. The identifiable excess is
metric-dependent under the full-size primary audit; continuous rank displacement
is retained only as a secondary magnitude diagnostic. A negative result is not
read as proof of no biology; it is a signal to inspect measurement,
model-member disagreement, metric geometry and implementation identity before
assigning a mechanism.

The Nadig registry and replication protect against a different failure mode:
choosing a favorable context pair after looking at risk. The result remains
bounded to one supported study design. The deployment probe likewise does not
turn heterogeneous transfer into a safety guarantee. A practical use of these
models should therefore report the raw ordering, its measurement plan, and the
reproducibility boundary together.

The monotonic-recalibration counterfactual is a supplementary lemma. A
train-only strictly increasing map can change Brier or log-loss while
preserving strict ordering and AURC, so it cannot repair a reordered reliability
surface. Full proof details and its 45-group audit are supplied in the
supplement rather than promoted to a main contribution.

\section{Limitations and reproducibility}
The claim is bounded by the eight-environment surface, three predictor families
in the headline Claim Lock and one Nadig same-predictor replication. The audit
cannot remove every laboratory or processing difference and identifies no
causal mechanism. Modern-model feasibility is documented separately from
scientific Claim Lock eligibility. PTL features remain outcome-free and
explicit-ID-free, but the source gate retains negative transfers.

All outputs are regenerated from fixed splits and native predictor spaces. The
release contains source-frozen vectors, full-size and split-half raw-cell
floors, tie-aware ordering rows, synchronized macro bootstrap draws, the complete outcome-blind registry, model
feasibility checks, transport matrices, bootstrap summaries, and editable
vector figures. Claims are downgraded when interval support, stability or
metric agreement is absent. The supplement provides the complete estimands,
registry fields, provenance decisions, secondary metrics and audit commands.
