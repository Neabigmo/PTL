Dear Editor,

Please consider our manuscript, "Reliability modeling of transportable perturbation responses in public single-cell functional genomics screens", for publication in Computational Biology and Chemistry.

This manuscript presents a computational biology study of reliability modeling for single-cell perturbation-response prediction. Using public Perturb-seq functional genomics screens, we show that random-split model rankings do not reliably transfer to held-out perturbations, combinations, low-support signatures, dataset-heldout settings, or an independent public experimental screen. We then evaluate the Perturbation Transportability Layer (PTL), a model-agnostic keep/abstain reliability model that reduces false transportability before downstream pathway interpretation and perturbation prioritization.

The work is aligned with the journal's scope in computational life sciences, functional genomics, bioinformatics, systems biology, and AI/machine learning for biological data. The manuscript emphasizes public-data grounded biological interpretation risk rather than presenting PTL as a wet-lab substitute or a tuned benchmark of any single perturbation predictor. It includes transparent baseline runs, public-screen holdouts, case examples of retained and rejected predictions, deterministic publication figures, and reproducible code and processed evidence tables.

The manuscript is original, has not been published, and is not under consideration elsewhere. The author declares no competing interests.
